Before you fly

Prepare

French citizens do not need a visa for a short visit, but everyone flying to Canada needs an eTA linked to their passport. Do this on the official government site only. Tick each step as you finish it.

1. eTA — do this first

An electronic travel authorization is required to board a flight to Canada. It costs CAD $7 per person, is valid for up to five years or until the passport expires, and is tied to that exact passport. If someone renews a passport after applying, they need a new eTA.

1

Use only the official site

Apply at Canada.ca. Ignore companies that charge extra to “help” you. The real fee is CAD $7.

2

Have these ready

Valid passport, an email address you check, and a credit or debit card. The form cannot be saved. Most approvals arrive within minutes; some take several days if extra documents are requested. Apply well before you fly.

3

Check the passport number

When the approval email arrives, confirm the passport number matches the physical passport. Bring that same passport to the airport. The airline will scan it; without a matching eTA you will not board.

3. Insurance & health

French Carte Vitale / EHIC coverage does not apply in Canada. Hospital care is expensive. Take a travel policy that covers medical treatment, cancellation, and the Niagara boat.

4. Bookings to confirm

Open each confirmation once, check names and times, and save a screenshot. The concert is not in the Distillery District — leave time to get to 115 Simpson Avenue.

5. Money, phones, time

  • Tell your bank you will be in Canada, or use a card that does not block foreign charges.
  • Currency is the Canadian dollar (CAD). Cards are widely accepted; keep a little cash for markets.
  • Restaurants usually expect a 15–20% tip if service is not already included.
  • Montréal and Toronto are 6 hours behind France in late October (EDT vs CEST).
  • Download offline maps for Montréal and Toronto. Québec is French-speaking; Toronto is English-speaking.
